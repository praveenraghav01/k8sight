1. Who we are & the scope of this policy

k8sight is an open-source desktop application distributed for macOS, Windows, and Linux. It is not a hosted service and has no user accounts. This policy applies to the desktop app and to this informational website. It does not apply to the third-party services you connect the app to (such as your Kubernetes clusters, Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, or an AI model provider you configure); your use of those services is governed by their own privacy policies and your agreements with them.

2. Our core principle: local-first, no collection

k8sight is architected so that we never see your data:

  • The app runs entirely on your local machine. There is no k8sight server, cloud service, or database that receives your information.
  • The app sends no telemetry, analytics, crash reports, usage statistics, or diagnostic data to us or to any third party.
  • The app requires no account, registration, sign-up, or login with us.
  • All processing — reading configuration, talking to clusters, rendering resources, generating auth tokens — happens locally on your device.

Because of this design, we do not "collect" personal data in the conventional sense. The sections below describe the data the app accesses locally on your behalf and the direct connections it makes to services you configure.

3. Information the app accesses (and where it stays)

To perform its function, the app reads and works with the following categories of data. In every case the data stays on your machine or travels directly between your machine and a service you configured — never through us.

DataWhy the app accesses itWhere it is stored / sent
Kubeconfig & cluster resources — contexts, cluster endpoints, and the Kubernetes objects you view (pods, deployments, services, logs, events, metrics, etc.) To display and let you operate your clusters. Read from your local kubeconfig (~/.kube/config). Cluster traffic goes directly between your machine and your Kubernetes API servers.
Cloud credentials & tokens — AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud access/refresh tokens or keys you provide when connecting clusters To discover your clusters and write kubeconfig entries so you can connect without a CLI. Stored locally (e.g. ~/.aws, ~/.kube, ~/.config/k8s-manager/) and used only to call that provider's APIs directly from your machine.
App settings & preferences — theme, chosen integrations, MCP read/write toggle, AI configuration To remember your choices between sessions. Stored locally in ~/.config/k8s-manager/settings.json and browser local storage.

We do not receive, transmit, or retain any of the above. If you never connect a cloud provider or sign in with Google, none of that data is ever accessed.

4. Google user data & the OAuth scopes we request

Connecting Google GKE clusters is optional. It is only used if you explicitly choose "Sign in with browser" for Google. When you do, k8sight uses Google OAuth 2.0 and requests the following scope:

Google OAuth scopeWhat k8sight does with it
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/cloud-platform (View and manage your Google Cloud data) Used only to (a) list the Google Cloud projects and Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE) clusters your account can access, and (b) obtain short-lived access tokens so the app and kubectl can authenticate to those clusters on your behalf. k8sight does not create, modify, or delete Google Cloud resources through this scope; it reads project/cluster metadata and mints authentication tokens.

The specific Google user data the app accesses is limited to: your Google account identity for the sign-in, the list of Google Cloud projects and GKE clusters visible to your account, and OAuth tokens (a refresh token and derived access tokens).

Storage and handling. The refresh token and derived credentials are stored only on your local device at ~/.config/k8s-manager/gke/credentials.json with file permissions restricted to your user account (mode 600). These tokens are never transmitted to us or to any third party; they are sent only to Google's own OAuth and API endpoints to authenticate you. We have no server that could receive them.

Revoking access. You can revoke k8sight's access to your Google account at any time by: signing out inside the app; deleting ~/.config/k8s-manager/gke/credentials.json; or visiting myaccount.google.com/permissions and removing k8sight. Revoking does not delete anything from your device automatically — remove the local file if you also want the local copy gone.

5. Limited Use disclosure

k8sight's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Specifically, information received from Google APIs is:

  • used only to provide and improve the cluster-connection features you explicitly invoke;
  • not transferred to or sold to third parties, except as necessary to provide or improve those features, to comply with applicable law, or as part of a merger, acquisition, or sale of assets with user notice;
  • not used or transferred for serving advertising, including retargeting, personalized, or interest-based advertising; and
  • not read by any human, unless (i) you give affirmative agreement for specific data, (ii) it is necessary for security purposes (such as investigating abuse), or (iii) it is required to comply with applicable law.

In practice, because k8sight has no servers, none of your Google user data ever reaches us — it stays on your device.

6. AWS and Azure sign-in

Connecting Amazon EKS or Azure AKS clusters is also optional and works the same local-first way. AWS sign-in (IAM Identity Center / SSO, access keys, or assume-role) and Azure sign-in (in-browser OAuth or the az CLI) authenticate directly with those providers. The resulting credentials and tokens are stored locally and used only to discover clusters and write kubeconfig entries on your machine. None of it is sent to us. Your use of AWS and Azure is governed by their respective privacy policies.

7. Optional AI assistant (bring your own model)

k8sight includes an optional AI assistant that is disabled until you configure it with your own OpenAI-compatible API endpoint and API key (for example OpenAI, Azure OpenAI, or a self-hosted gateway such as LiteLLM or TrueFoundry). If you enable and use it:

  • Relevant, read-only cluster context needed to answer your question is sent directly to the endpoint you chose — never to us.
  • Values that look like secrets are redacted before the request is sent.
  • Your API key is stored locally on your device.

That exchange is governed by the privacy policy of the AI provider you selected. We are not a party to it and cannot see the content.

8. How data is stored & secured

All data the app uses is stored on your own device using the operating system's file system and standard locations (~/.kube, ~/.aws, ~/.config/k8s-manager/). Sensitive credential files, such as Google tokens, are written with restrictive file permissions (owner-only, mode 600). Because we operate no servers, there is no central store of user data for us to secure or for an attacker to breach through us. The security of the data on your device also depends on your own machine's security (disk encryption, account protection, and OS updates), which is under your control.

9. How we share data

We do not sell, rent, trade, or share your personal data or Google user data with anyone, because we never receive it in the first place. The only "sharing" that occurs is the app, running on your device, communicating directly with the services you configure (your Kubernetes clusters, AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, or your chosen AI endpoint) to perform the actions you request. We are not an intermediary in those connections.

10. Data retention & deletion

Since we hold no data, there is nothing for us to retain or delete on our side. On your device, you remain in full control and can delete data at any time by:

  • signing out of a provider inside the app;
  • deleting the relevant local files (for example ~/.config/k8s-manager/, cloud credential files under ~/.aws, or kubeconfig entries in ~/.kube/config); or
  • uninstalling the app.

11. Your rights & choices

Because all data stays on your device and we collect nothing, you already have direct and complete control over it. You can access, modify, export, or delete any of it using your own file system and the in-app sign-out controls, without needing to make a request to us. If you have connected Google, you can additionally revoke access as described in Section 4. Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA; since we do not process your personal data, most such requests are satisfied by your own local controls, but you may still contact us with questions (Section 16).

12. The website

The k8sight website is a static informational site. It does not require an account and does not set advertising or cross-site tracking cookies. The site may load a third-party "Buy Me a Coffee" support widget; if you choose to interact with it, Buy Me a Coffee processes that interaction under its own privacy policy. Some pages include product videos hosted on YouTube. Nothing is loaded from YouTube until you press play; the player then loads from YouTube’s privacy-enhanced domain (youtube-nocookie.com) and YouTube handles that playback under its own privacy policy. Standard, short-lived server request logs kept by our hosting provider for reliability and security are not used by us to build profiles of visitors or for advertising.

13. Children's privacy

k8sight is a developer and operations tool intended for professional use. It is not directed to, and we do not knowingly collect information from, children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your jurisdiction).

14. International users

k8sight is a locally installed application. Any connections it makes go directly from your device to the services you configure, which may be located in various countries. We do not control where those third-party services process data; please review their policies. We ourselves receive and transfer no user data.
